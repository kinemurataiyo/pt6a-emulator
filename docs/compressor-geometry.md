# Compressor geometry and rotation

The supplied United Turbine PT6 Training Manual defines engine directions as viewed from the rear with the accessory case facing the viewer (page 2). Its Compressor Turbine Rotor Assembly section states that the compressor turns counterclockwise (page 18). In this model the rear is +X, so that rotation is +Rx. It appears clockwise when viewed from the opposite, propeller end.

The three axial rotors, centrifugal impeller, compressor turbine, and gas-generator shaft share that rotation. The corrected axial rotor stagger and camber transfer motion into air travelling from the rear inlet toward the impeller (-X), adding tangential swirl. The following stationary stators have the opposite stagger and turn that flow back toward the axial direction. The compressor turbine retains its energy-extracting blade orientation. The power-turbine and propeller assembly remains independent and turns in the opposite direction.

Dense repeating blade rows can appear to turn backward when a rendered frame advances by more than half a blade spacing. The gas-generator study animation limits each displayed step to 40% of the smallest rotating gas-row spacing (40 compressor-turbine blades). Ordinary 1x motion at 60 FPS is unchanged; at slow frame rates or faster study settings the display slows to preserve a readable direction. This limit does not change simulated Ng, power, or governor behavior.

The centrifugal impeller is reconstructed from the user's supplied reference photograph: a dished backplate, raised central inlet, and separate curved vanes with open channels and radial discharge. The inlet face points toward +X. Selecting the impeller focuses the camera on that face; Isolate exposes it without the upstream compressor rows. The manual's Compressor Rotor and Stator Assembly and Diffuser Assembly sections establish the single centrifugal stage and downstream diffuser arrangement. The photograph guides the visible shape; neither source establishes the model's exact vane count, dimensions, or airfoil calibration.

Regression checks use the actual rendered axial blade sections and animation direction to confirm that blade motion pushes air into the core and adds the expected swirl. They also cover stationary stators, shaft synchronization, low-frame-rate display direction, and open impeller passages. These geometric checks establish consistent visual direction, not a calibrated aerodynamic solution.
